# Property Details 2

**Source type:** kb_entry

**Product Features**

The property. 1,000 acres of private Hill Country land outside Johnson City, Texas, about nine minutes from the town center and fifty minutes from central Austin. The property has been in continuous family ownership for over 150 years, one of the earliest families to settle in the area. Roughly 100 acres along the Pedernales River are under a conservation easement held by the Nature Conservancy. The family has conducted wildlife surveys for endangered species, removed cattle to let the land rest, and plans to bring them back under a regenerative grazing program.

**The land.**

The visual character of the property is defined by the contrast between vibrant green vegetation and white limestone. The terrain includes juniper forest, meadows dotted with wildflowers in spring, ridge lines, and spring-fed creeks running throughout. The property has hundreds of yards of direct river frontage on the Pedernales River with large flat-top limestone rocks for laying out, shallow shelves where you can set lounge chairs directly in the water, and deep pools for swimming. Wildlife on the property includes coyotes, armadillos, white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, and the golden-cheeked warbler, a federally endangered bird protected by the conservation easement. There is a specific location known to the family as the Turkey Spot where wild turkeys frequently show up. The family has always kept dogs at the ranch, most recently a German Shorthaired Pointer.

**The wildflower season.**

The wildflowers don’t all come at once. They move through waves over about six weeks. Bluebonnets come first. Then multicolored pinks and yellows. Then reds.

**The cabins.**

Architecturally designed permanent structures cantilevered off a hillside, built from cedar and limestone with pitched roofs and glass walls. The pitched roof is a traditional form that keeps the structure from reading as too modern or too spaceship. The cantilever orientation is intentional. The cabin pushes guests forward and out over the landscape when they’re in the front living space, with glass walls looking out and down, then nestles them back into the trees in the bedroom where the cabin connects to the land. Each cabin has a limestone and gravel courtyard at the entry level with native grasses and plants. A wall along the road side provides privacy from the parking area. The courtyard grounds the arrival at a stilted structure so it doesn’t feel like you’re walking up to something floating above you. Guests enter through a ramp along the side of the cabin. As they walk down the ramp, the view opens up in front of them before they turn left and enter the cabin.

The front living space has glass walls facing the westward views.

There is a reading nook carved out and jutting from the main footprint. The bedroom sits back where the cabin connects to the hillside, tucked into the trees. The cabin includes a kitchenette, a fire pit as part of the outdoor space, and espresso and coffee machines. The space underneath the stilted cabins between the steel beams and the ground is open and can be landscaped or built out as usable outdoor space.

The property launches with one cabin and will add a small number more. Low unit count is a permanent operating decision, not a phase.
