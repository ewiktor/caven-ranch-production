# Marsha's List 2

**Source type:** kb_entry

## Animals:

**White-tailed Deer** – Most visible animal in the Hill Country. Some epic antlers on the bucks and mythologies around finding the one “big one” in the area

**Rio Grande Wild Turkey** – Turkeys are really smart and travel in packs. It’s rare to see them because they are so good at avoiding danger. You can often hear them gobble but a sighting is a treat.

**Golden-cheeked Warbler** – Endemic and endangered; relies on Ashe juniper for nesting. Only place in the world where they are found. We have confirmed sightings on our property

**Nine-banded Armadillo** – Texas icon; nocturnal and unmistakable. Rolls up to protect itself which is pretty cool.

**Monarch Butterfly** - They migrate straight through the Hill Country on their way to Mexico for the winter.

**Western Diamondback Rattlesnake** – Spooky, these guys can mess you up with a bit but they are icon to the American West

**Mexican Free-tailed Bat** – Famous Hill Country bat species; critical insect control.

**Guadalupe bass** - State fish of Texas and found in the Pedernales river. Classic Bass striping on the side

**Texas Plains Coyote** - Travel in groups at night and can make some incredible noise. Part of their defense mechanism is that a few can make their how;s sound like many. Can be eerie but enchanting to listen to them at night in the distance.

**Greater Roadrunner** - These are fun to see and are often sprinting across the roads out at the ranch.

## Plants:

**Texas Live Oak** – The defining tree of the Hill Country; ancient, sprawling, and epic when they get old and large.

**Ashe Juniper (Cedar)** – Ubiquitous evergreen shaping the region’s silhouette and scent. Some controversy about whether they are even native to the region, but at this point they are by far the most common tree you will see in the Hill Country. They provide a lot of habitat, and a lot of pollen for allergy sufferers.

**Texas Mountain Laurel** – Beautiful evergreen shrub with little grape-scented purple blooms each spring.

**Texas Bluebonnet** – The one and only, this one needs no introduction. The Bluebonnet is the most legendary plant in the Hill Country. It is the state flower; an iconic spring wildflower along roads and fields. It only lasts a couple of weeks during the spring wildflower season.

**Prickly Pear Cactus** – Classic cactus of limestone soils; edible pads and fruit. Can grow to be epic in size. Lots of cocktails, juices etc are made out of the fruit which is a bright pink/red. Can have beautiful yellow and red blooms as well. Super thorny and will hurt a ton if you fall into one.

**Texas Sage (Cenizo)** – Silvery foliage and dramatic purple blooms after rain. Can be really beautiful when they are large and blooming

**Bald Cypress** – Majestic riparian tree lining the Pedernales River. We have a huge collection of them where Miller Creek meets the Pedernales. These things are massive and ancient.

**Texas Pecan** – Native tree along waterways; culturally and economically important as pecans feature in a lot of recipes from the region.

**Little Bluestem** – Native prairie grass turning copper-red in fall. Native grasses are getting more attention for the critical importance in regulating soil health and restoring the region to it’s pre-settler ecological glory. This is the king of them all (not to be confused with King Ranch Bluestem which was introduced to the region and is invasive and sucks)

**Texas Indian Paintbrush** - Another wildflower that comes out in the spring just after the Bluebonnets. Pretty red flowers that are small on a climbing stalk.
