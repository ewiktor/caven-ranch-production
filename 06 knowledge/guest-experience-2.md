# Guest Experience 2

**Source type:** kb_entry

**The drive and arrival.**

The drive from Austin is about fifty minutes. It transitions from a four-lane state highway to a two-lane winding backroad about halfway through. When you arrive you turn off the road and go up a long drive that is the panhandle of the property reaching out to the road. You hit a first gate before going further in as the property opens up behind the pieces of land closer to the road that got sold off from the original 5,000 acres over the years. The road veers left toward the sweeping westward views, still hidden by the tree line at that point. A road has been cut that drops gently down from the property high point about 100 feet before a sharp right turns the road parallel to the rough limestone face of the ridge. The drive down this road to the cabins is a tunnel of foliage through the Ashe Juniper forest where the cabins were placed. The view is mostly hidden until the guest arrives at their cabin, gets out, and starts heading for the ramp. That’s when the landscape opens up.

**The daily rhythm.**

The best way to experience Texas for most of the year follows a pattern. You are outside in the morning and in the evening. The middle of the day, roughly 11 AM to 3 PM, is too hot to be out comfortably. Mornings are slow and quiet. Wake up at first light, watch the sunrise, make coffee with the espresso machine and take it out to the deck or down to the patio. Sit in the sun and listen to the birds.

The afternoon heat is for the river or for being inside. At the river you set up towels on the wide flat limestone rocks, put lounge chairs in the shallow shelves, and use the deeper pools for swimming or just floating with a drink. Inside is for reading, napping, or heading into town.

Mid to late afternoon is wine and cheese or cocktails and crackers, good for shaded time during the heat window. Around 4 or 5 PM the temperature drops and there’s a burst of energy and a few good hours as the sun sets. Golden hour is for gathering on the patio with wine or cocktails.

Nights are for stargazing and sitting by the fire pit. The sky transitions from deep orange sunset to cool evening haze to dark night sky with stars. No light pollution. The warmth of being in the cabin at night with a lamp on, looking out through the glass at the dark landscape, is a specific experience. The contrast between the warm interior and the dark outside.

The sound changes through the day too. Birds in the morning. Quiet during the heat. Bugs and crickets at dusk. The light. The light on the property changes through the day. Morning is soft. Midday is bright and flat. The sunset hours produce deep oranges and warm golds. Night is dark, no light pollution, full of stars.

**Off-property.**

Guests can take day trips to Johnson City and surrounding towns. Johnson City is a small town with a main square built around the Blanco County Courthouse, same layout as almost every small Texas town. Old buildings around the square have been repurposed over the years, original signage still on them.

Pecan Street Brewing is in a restored 1950s hardware store on the Johnson City town square. They kept the original gold-leaf lettering on the windows. Bryan’s on 290 is the more elevated dinner option, seasonal menu, locally sourced, speakeasy atmosphere. Herencia Cuisine is a French food truck run by Chef Christophe in the middle of Johnson City. The bakery down the road offers cooking classes.

OroBianco Italian Creamery is in downtown Blanco, about 20 minutes south. First and only water buffalo dairy and creamery in Texas. Roughly 400 water buffalo raised on ranches in south Texas and the Hill Country, milked on grass-only diets with no hormones or antibiotics. Small-batch gelato from water buffalo milk, fresh mozzarella, ricotta, feta, and cured meats made by hand. The owner, Phil Giglio, was a corporate attorney in Chicago and New York before moving to Texas.

Highway 290 between Johnson City and Fredericksburg is called Wine Road. Over 50 wineries along the corridor. The Hill Country is the second most visited wine destination in the country after Napa Valley. Tempranillo, a Spanish varietal, grows well here because of the limestone soil and climate. The Hill Country sits on a limestone formation that shares geological characteristics with wine regions in the south of France and central Spain. A family friend who grew up near the Pyrenees visited the ranch and said the landscape felt like home.

Pedernales Falls State Park is about 10 miles east of Johnson City. The Pedernales River cascades over layered limestone steps. The park covers 5,200 acres. The river limestone is 300 million years old.

The LBJ National Historical Park and LBJ Boyhood Home are in Johnson City. The Texas White House is nearby. Johnson himself was not related to the Johnson family the town was named for. He grew up there and claimed the association throughout his political career.

Texas Hill Country Olive Company is nearby with olive oil tastings and a retail shop. The Science Mill is a family-friendly interactive museum in Johnson City housed in a converted 1880s cotton mill. The experience. No programming, no scheduled activities, no staff managing guest time. The property provides the place and the land. What guests do with it is up to them. Stays are self-directed. The property includes curated local itineraries for the surrounding Hill Country. Nightly rates are in the $400-500 range. Special experiences. On-property experiences like days at the Outpost or open flame dinners. A safari tent is being built where guests can have lunch in a different part of the property.
