# Layered overlap — expressions doc

**Version:** V1 · 2026-07-17 · New expression off the 2026-07-17 reference batch (illustration-overlapping-background-split, split-screen-layout-with-card-overlay, split-layout-with-overlapping-illustration, historical-ephemera-web-layout, layered-list-with-overlapping-imagery, split-layout-with-serif-headline)
**Type:** brand expression (recurring compositional device)

## What it is

An element — an illustration, an image, a card, or a historical object like a letter — crosses a boundary: it overlaps text, breaks out of the frame of a color block, or bridges two color fields at once, like a coyote silhouette cutting across the split between forest-green and cream. The move exists to add depth and a "put-together, human" feeling — the antidote to flat text-sitting-on-white. It shows up as a line-art illustration laid over a headline, a card overlaid on one half of a split-screen image, a photo partially obscuring a list, or the brand name "Caven" set directly over a photograph, so the layers read as assembled by a person rather than dropped into a grid.

## What the user likes

The cutout that lives in two planes at once: "I like how the illustrations are a cutout from the colored background and the white background... Could potentially be like a split screen thing where the illustration cuts the split." The illustration crossing over the headline: "the illustration that goes over the text on the left with a nice image on the right. It could even say 'Haven' over the image on the right" — the brand name integrated straight into the photo. The card-on-split-screen: "I do like the split-screen image thing with a card overlay... imagine it's another card overlay with more information, or use an illustration." The overlap that only half-reveals: "the image going over the text. You don't fully see it, but get that little bit of a human, put-together feeling of things." And the historical letter as a layered object: "You can imagine this letter laying on top of an image, and it's like a historical element on the website." One consistent read across all six: the layout and the overlap are what he wants; the paper/archival texture and the text density are what he pushes off.

## Control for (locked)

- **Composition (layering / depth):** at least one element crosses a boundary — overlapping text, breaking a color-block frame, or bridging two color fields — so planes read stacked, not flat — provenance: user, "the illustration that goes over the text," "the image going over the text... that little bit of a human, put-together feeling" (split-layout-with-overlapping-illustration, layered-list-with-overlapping-imagery)
- **The cutout-across-the-split:** a single silhouette/illustration masked so it sits in both the colored field and the cream field at once, unifying a split — provenance: user, "the illustrations are a cutout from the colored background and the white background... the illustration cuts the split" (illustration-overlapping-background-split)
- **Intent (feel):** depth and a human, assembled, "put-together" quality — the deliberate antidote to text-on-plain-white — provenance: user, "that little bit of a human, put-together feeling of things" (layered-list-with-overlapping-imagery)
- **Type in the overlap:** where type sits over image or is crossed by an element, it stays ink / near-black, mixed case, never uppercase, never colored with a palette accent — provenance: settled.md ("Type is never colored... Type = ink (near-black)"; no-uppercase rule)

## Scenarios (build worlds, judged against each other)

- **What crosses the boundary:** the device is one move with several bodies — (a) a line-art or coyote illustration over a headline; (b) an image/photo overlapping a text list; (c) a card overlaid on one half of a split-screen image; (d) a historical letter/ephemera object laid over a photo; (e) the brand name "Caven" set directly over a photograph. The historical-object world carries a real constraint: it must read contemporary, the letter as a designed layered element, NOT as scanned aged paper (see Negative). Build the illustration-crossing world and the card-overlay world at minimum; judge as worlds.

## Leave open — filled + cycled (variation space)

- **Which boundary gets crossed (focus control — forks to build distinct):** a color-block frame, the split between two color fields, or a text block. The split-bridging cutout ([10 split-screen layout](../10%20split-screen%20layout/doc.md)) and the frame-break ([18 image framing](../18%20image%20framing/doc.md)) are genuinely different structures — build both, don't average.
- **The overlapping element's medium:** coyote silhouette, line-art illustration, photograph, card, or letter — cycle by content concept; illustrations pull from [02 illustration language](../02%20illustration%20language/doc.md), a crossing coyote from [01 coyote character](../01%20coyote%20character/doc.md) / [22 coyote motion sequence](../22%20coyote%20motion%20sequence/doc.md).
- **How much the overlap reveals vs. hides:** the user likes only partly seeing the layered image — cycle the degree of occlusion; the "put-together" read comes from not fully revealing.
- **Split-screen relationship:** which half carries the card, whether the card is a menu-like info panel or an illustration — filled against [10 split-screen layout](../10%20split-screen%20layout/doc.md) and [11 offset stack layout](../11%20offset%20stack%20layout/doc.md).
- **Color fields:** the green-and-cream split the ref shows is one instance; cycle within the active color direction ([05 palette](../05%20palette/doc.md), one direction at random per update per settled.md).
- **Negative space around the overlap:** the crossing reads best against open space — coordinate with [04 negative space layout](../04%20negative%20space%20layout/doc.md).

## Negative

- **No aged / scanned paper texture.** The user likes the card-overlay and the ephemera layouts but rejects the substrate: "I don't like the paper background, but I do like the layout"; and the whole brand is off archival/old-paper. The historical letter must be a contemporary designed object, not a scanned artifact. — provenance: user (split-screen-layout-with-card-overlay) + settled.md (Direction, contemporary/never archival)
- **Not text-heavy.** The overlap carries the interest; copy stays restrained. The user twice flags density: "A little bit too much text here on this one." — provenance: user (split-layout-with-serif-headline)
- **Not two competing illustrations in one composition.** "I wouldn't use two illustrations like this." — provenance: user (illustration-overlapping-background-split)
- **Never uppercase, never green type.** — provenance: settled.md.
