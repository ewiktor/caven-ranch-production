# Illustration language — expressions doc

**Version:** V2 · 2026-07-16 · Amended same-session with the user's Expressions Catalogue v4 (Notion): named style directions, the ISO principle, land-specificity directive, prototype-round findings
**Type:** brand expression (graphics / illustration system)

## What it is

The hand-made drawing language of the brand: animals, ranch subjects, and land forms rendered in gestural ink, brushed linocut positive-space, painterly brushwork, etched cross-hatch, and print-textured styles. It spans static illustration, oversized page-level graphics (the Ett Hem move: a big illustration as the primary graphic of a page), and motion (a galloping pack as preloader, ghosted sequential frames). It exists so the brand's graphic surfaces feel made by hand on this land — and because illustration is currently the strongest path for avoiding AI-looking output, per the user's early testing.

## What the user likes

Expressive economy: "loose gestural ink marks prioritize movement and personality over exact anatomy," detail concentrated where it matters and paper left open; "confident pen work where the line has real character — not clean vector, not careless scribble." Bold shapes over thin outlines: "forms are built with bold, confident shapes… internal texturing achieved by leaving areas of the paper open." Subjects sitting small in heavy white space. Visible making — variable pressure, dry-brush, stippling, impasto fur, halftone grain, print-proof residue. From the prototype round that didn't land: the failure poles are "too detailed" and "too thin and generic" — the small navy-ink cowboy on horseback with a single ground line was the closest hit.

## Control for (locked)

- **Subject/Illustration:** hand-made mark quality; anatomical precision under expressive marks; selective detail, open surrounding paper; subjects can sit small in the frame
- **Texture:** visible paper grain, distressed edges, stipple and cross-hatch, halftone dithering, print-proof artifacts (crop marks, registration), dry-brush imperfection kept intentional
- **Mood:** crafted, restrained, unpretentious; heritage print-shop lineage; energetic but controlled
- **Land specificity (directive, from the user's catalogue):** landscape illustration reflects the ranch's actual topography — flat riverbeds, limestone shelves, horizontal strata of water over rock, scrubby low vegetation
- **The ISO principle (directive):** every scene-based style must preserve the ability to isolate individual characters/elements to stand alone on an open page
- **Placement (from the user's layout philosophy):** illustrations float in frame with soft/faded edges, not hard-edged rectangles

## Leave open — filled + cycled (variation space)

- **Style fork (focus control — the user's own named directions plus the reference modes; build as distinct variations):**
  - (a) **Toby Dog** — gestural editorial ink, weight-varied line, pub-sign/heritage-print-shop lineage; the sweet spot sits between the wilder Toby pole and the tighter badge-like Green Rabbit pole (user's open tension)
  - (b) **General brand ink/linocut** — high-contrast positive-space, single ink color on textured paper, brushed/linocut process ("the primary illustrative voice" in the catalogue)
  - (c) **Negative space / subtraction** — subject as void or cutout against a solid brand color, brushed silhouette edges, detail via subtractive marks
  - (d) **Painterly color-field** — directional brushstrokes, contrasting fields instead of outlines (the dalmatian ref)
  - (e) **Etched line** — cross-hatch/stipple (greyhound, western canine); white ink on deep dark ground as its inverse
  - (f) **Shibori / embroidered** — stitch-textured characters, optional indigo dip-dye ground; connected to botanical/ecology content; characters work standalone without the dyed ground
  - (g) **Risograph** — halftone grain, limited off-register palette, 60s/70s animation feel; **a capsule, not the whole voice** — reserved for the Lost Hunter storyline per the user
- **Ground:** open paper, watercolor wash, textile, dark navy field, solid brand color — cycle
- **Motion:** sequential/layered frames communicating time (ghosted overlaps); the horizontal galloping pack (feeds [08 preloader](../08%20preloader/doc.md))
- **Subject matter (filled):** from `06 knowledge/` and `03 media/` — the animal character, horses, flora and fauna from Marsha's record, land forms, ranch objects; nothing invented

## Negative

- **Generic mountain tropes and vertical peaks** in landscape illustration — ruled out by the user's land-specificity directive (Expressions Catalogue v4).
- **The first prototype round's failure poles** — "too detailed (the cowboy, the oaks)" and "too thin and generic (the river lines)" — named by the user as what didn't land.

## New references — 2026-07-17 (bake-in)

These two open a **contemporary illustration path** — one that reads as designed, not archival — and also seed new expression [24 marshas list identity](../24%20marshas%20list%20identity/doc.md) (cross-reference; do not duplicate that work here).

- [stamped-fabric-gyotaku-illustration](../../04%20references/stamped-fabric-gyotaku-illustration.md) — "It doesn't look like paper. It looks more like fabric and more like it's stamped on fabric." A stamp/print-on-fabric register, subject cropped and continued across the frame (back half top, front half bottom). New open direction: an ink-on-fabric stamped illustration mode where imperfection is the point — "I like how the text is not perfect, so I'm following the fabric." Subject-agnostic: "whatever it is that's pressed on this fabric."
- [hand-drawn-sequential-illustration-layout](../../04%20references/hand-drawn-sequential-illustration-layout.md) — "the illustrations, and there are multiple illustrations with different frames of that illustration." Fine-liner / blue-ballpoint linework, a set of small illustrations of area places or times of day ("stars, fishes, the river... getting to the cabins, like different parts of the day"). New open direction: sequential small-illustration series as a layout system. Kills carried from the ref: "I don't like the red dots on the left"; and the price labels ("we wouldn't do the prices").
