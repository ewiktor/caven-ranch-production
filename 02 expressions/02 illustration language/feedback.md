> "Visible directional brushstrokes model the animal's anatomy, while contrasting color fields replace hard outlines. Impasto-like fur, a matte orange ground, and cool gray-blue shading combine dimensional rendering with graphic flatness." — textured-painterly-dalmatian-illustration

> "Loose gestural ink marks prioritize movement and personality over exact anatomy. Variable weight, over-sketching, cross-hatching, and dense ink clusters concentrate detail in character interactions while leaving the surrounding paper open." — minimalist-ink-line-illustration

> "White pigment is drawn over a deep navy textured ground. Variable pressure, dry-brush marks, translucent layers, and intentional imperfections create a raw but controlled illustration style focused on essential silhouettes and type." — gestural-white-ink-line-illustration

> "A synchronized group of animals in a horizontal gallop, intended as a reference for a motion-based preloader. The vintage, painterly treatment uses textured shading and anatomical precision. The overlapping pack could be adapted into a group of coyotes." — galloping-animal-pack-illustration-style

> "Sequential horse frames are layered at different opacities to communicate motion and time. Halftone dithering, ghosted frames, crop marks, registration details, textured paper, and overlapping type create a print-proof aesthetic." — sublimated-layered-frame-graphics

> "Expressive charcoal-like marks are combined with block-printed or stenciled pattern overlays. Gritty stippling, distressed edges, selective anatomy, and visible paper grain create a tactile mixed-media treatment." — textured-linework-greyhound-illustration

> "Colored pencil and gouache-like textures create tactile, overlapping horse forms. Delicate outlines, directional hatching, stippling, flattened perspective, and rhythmic repetition give the composition depth without heavy contouring." — textured-mixed-media-equine-illustration

From the user's Expressions Catalogue v4 (Notion, months old — "use as you will" per the user 2026-07-16), verbatim:

> "Gestural editorial ink illustration with a hand-drawn, slightly loose quality. Think traditional pub signage, heritage print shops, the kind of confident pen work where the line has real character — not clean vector, not careless scribble… The line has weight variation, a slight roughness, and reads as authentically drawn rather than generated. Heavy white space. Subjects tend to sit small in the frame." — Direction 1 "Toby Dog"

> "The first round of prototypes (PDF) were guided by this reference but didn't land — they came out either too detailed (the cowboy, the oaks) or too thin and generic (the river lines). The Toby dog and Green Rabbit references aren't perfectly consistent with each other stylistically — the Toby is wilder and more gestural, the Green Rabbit is tighter and more badge-like. Need to find the sweet spot between those two poles. The small navy-ink cowboy on horseback with the single ground line is the closest prototype to actually hitting this." — Direction 1 notes

> "[T]his defines the primary illustrative voice for the brand, applied to various subjects such as flora, fauna, and ranch objects. It is defined by a high-contrast, positive-space technique using a single ink color on a textured paper background. The aesthetic mimics a brushed ink or linocut process, where forms are built with bold, confident shapes rather than thin outlines." — General Brand Style

> "The subject is rendered as a void or cutout against a solid brand color background (e.g., Terracotta or Deep River Blue). The silhouette avoids clean digital vector edges in favor of a brushed, hand-drawn quality. Internal details are suggested strictly through subtractive marks." — Negative Space / Subtraction

> "This is NOT the whole brand voice. The brand is refined and serious. This direction is specifically for the 'Lost Hunter' storyline… It's a capsule within the brand world." — Direction 2 "Risograph"

> "Need to preserve the ability to isolate individual characters/elements (the 'ISO' principle). Sometimes you just need the cowboy by himself, without the full scene." — Direction 2 notes

> "Unlike the risograph direction, the embroidered characters could potentially work as standalone elements WITHOUT the dyed background. The stitch texture is intrinsic to the character itself." — Direction 3 "Shibori / Embroidered"

> "This directive ensures that all landscape illustrations accurately reflect the specific unique topography of the ranch, such as the flat riverbeds with distinct limestone shelves and the horizontal layers of water flowing over rock. It grounds the visual identity in the actual place by strictly avoiding generic mountain tropes or vertical peaks." — Land Specificity

> "Illustrations float in frame with soft/faded edges, not hard-edged rectangles. Reference: Ett Hem website — illustrations and photography coexisting, simple structure, lots of air." — Layout Philosophy (background context)
