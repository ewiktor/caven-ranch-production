# Coyote character — expressions doc

**Version:** V2 · 2026-07-16 · Amended same-session with the user's Creative Concepts doc (Notion): never-announced rule, coyote-vs-GSP fork, illustration-leads finding, the break-out intent
**Type:** brand expression (recurring brand character / subject)

## What it is

A recurring animal character treated with exaggerated seriousness — the wild animal of this land given the formal portrait treatment usually reserved for people. It shows up across the brand in different forms — a studio photograph in one place, an illustration on another page, an animation in the preloader — and **is never explained, announced, or labeled as a mascot. It just appears**, and people start to notice it without being told to look for it. It exists to give the brand a voice rooted in the actual fauna of the property while refusing the cute-mascot and Western-kitsch defaults — one attempt to break out of the "every other Western hotel brand" round that got killed.

## What the user likes

The tension between formality and absurdity: "the absurdity of putting a wild or working animal into a composed, studio-type setting against a brand that is otherwise grounded and rooted in 150 years of family history." A coyote shot like a mid-century encyclopedia plate, or cropped so extreme (ear-and-eye, snout macro) it becomes abstract pattern — "a high-fashion anti-portrait." The caught-in-the-act rawness of trail-cam flash (the origin of the idea, connected to actual coyotes on the property). When illustrated, the animal stays "unexpected but dignified." The brand "wants to be edgy and bold in specific ways" — Nude Project is the reference for the *willingness* to do bold work with good art direction, not for the look. Illustration may be the strongest path for avoiding AI-looking output, per the user's early testing.

## Control for (locked)

- **Brand (presence rule):** the character is never announced, labeled, or explained anywhere it appears — no "meet our mascot," no name treatment, no callout; it just appears
- **Mood:** deadpan, formal, dignified; exaggerated seriousness with an absurdist undercurrent; edgy and bold in specific ways, never wacky
- **Imagery (treatment):** studio isolation on flat saturated fields; extreme crops that break portrait convention; vintage-film grain, halftone texture; harsh direct flash with motion blur for the trail-cam mode; hyper-detailed fur texture
- **Subject:** the animal — singular character or pack; anatomical precision underneath whatever stylization sits on top

## Scenarios

- **Which animal:** the coyote (actual coyotes live on the property) **or the family's German Shorthaired Pointer** (the family has always kept GSPs on the property) — the user holds this genuinely open; both have a real connection to the ranch. Variations get built per world where the species reads: same treatment, different animal, judged as worlds. No build dependency.

## Leave open — filled + cycled (variation space)

- **Rendering world (focus control — the hedges are forks, build as distinct variations):** (a) studio anti-portrait photography on saturated ground; (b) trail-cam flash capture; (c) painterly/abstract illustration, domestic/stylized setting; (d) mid-century encyclopedia portrait with serif captioning. The user's read: "the right answer is probably a mix of forms depending on context," with illustration currently the strongest single path.
- **Color of the isolation field:** saturated red, desaturated green, flat blue all appear in the references — cycle; relationship to the brand palette ([05 palette](../05%20palette/doc.md)) is context, not a constraint
- **Scale of presence:** hero subject vs. small recurring detail (stamp, margin figure, pattern repeat — the western canine ref suggests pattern application)
- **Structural role in the brand:** "either as something that ties the whole creative direction together centrally or as a bold, unique design concept that gets deployed across the brand in different ways" — the user's own fork, unresolved

## Negative

- **Old Western large-format camera style** — explored and killed: "felt too old" (user, Creative Concepts doc).
