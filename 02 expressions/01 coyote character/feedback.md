From the user's Creative Concepts doc (Notion, "Creative Concepts (1)", loaded 2026-07-16), verbatim:

> "The coyote shows up across the brand in different forms and is never explained or announced. It just appears. A studio photograph in one place, an illustration on another page, an animation in the preloader. People start to notice it without being told to look for it. It is never labeled or called out as a brand mascot or symbol."

> "The idea came from trail cam photography, the kind hunters set up that captures wildlife at night with high flash, which connected to the actual coyotes on the property. Whether the animal is a coyote or the family's German Shorthaired Pointer is still open. Both have a real connection to the ranch. The family has always kept GSPs on the property."

> "The appeal is the absurdity of putting a wild or working animal into a composed, studio-type setting against a brand that is otherwise grounded and rooted in 150 years of family history. The brand is not stiff or conservative, though. It wants to be edgy and bold in specific ways. The Austin audience is creative, entrepreneurial, and drawn to brands that do something unexpected. Nude Project is a reference point for that willingness to just do bold shit with good art direction, not for the specific look or demographic."

> "The forms the coyote could take are still being figured out. Studio photography with the animal in a controlled setting. Painterly or abstract illustration. Trail cam style. Old Western large-format camera style was explored but felt too old. Illustration may be the strongest path for avoiding AI-looking output, per early testing and feedback. The right answer is probably a mix of forms depending on context."

> "The previous round of brand explorations felt like every other Western hotel brand. The coyote is one attempt to break out of that, either as something that ties the whole creative direction together centrally or as a bold, unique design concept that gets deployed across the brand in different ways."
