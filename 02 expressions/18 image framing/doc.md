# Image framing — expressions doc

**Version:** V1 · 2026-07-16 · Split out of the first pass's "editorial layout" at the user's direction to make the layout systems concrete
**Type:** brand expression (layout / graphic treatment)

## What it is

How single images and graphics are contained and interrupted: hard insets with mats and hairline rules, picture-in-picture cut-outs that reveal a second perspective, graphics breaking across a photograph's corner, tape and paper artifacts holding images to the page, and illustrations floating with soft faded edges instead of hard rectangles. The containment system every layout expression borrows from.

## What the user likes

Disruption with discipline: "a rectangular inset interrupts a primary subject or silhouette to reveal a second, more detailed perspective… a picture-in-picture effect that disrupts conventional spatial relationships." The 2D/photo handshake: "a saturated blue vector illustration breaks across the lower-left corner of a rectangular photograph… creating depth and interaction between two-dimensional and photographic layers." Tape texture as an honest fastener. For illustration: soft/faded floating edges, never hard-edged rectangles (the user's layout philosophy).

## Control for (locked)

- **Containment discipline:** every image is deliberately held — mat, hairline rule, bordered inset, seam-crosser, or explicit float; nothing just placed
- **Illustration edges:** soft/faded floats, not hard rectangles (user directive)
- **Interruptions:** cut-outs and corner-breakers are composed moves with a reading purpose, used sparingly

## Leave open — filled + cycled (variation space)

- **Device fork (focus control):** (a) plate/mat with hairline rules (the archival register); (b) inset picture-in-picture cut-out; (c) graphic breaking the photo corner; (d) tape/paper-fastener artifact; (e) soft-edge float — cycle
