# Brief — social · character sightings (expression 01 coyote character)

Built through the L-steps. Concept: `07 content concepts/social - character sightings.md`. Expression: `02 expressions/01 coyote character/`. Facts from `06 knowledge/property-details-2.md` (coyotes live on the property; trail cameras; the river and the low-water crossing; the Turkey Spot).

## L2 — editorial outline

Reconcile: the concept wants an unlabeled, recurring character built in public. The knowledge supports it plainly: coyotes are on the property, the whole idea started from trail-camera captures, and there are real named places (the river crossing, the Turkey Spot). No fact needs inventing. The one thing the knowledge does not give is a coyote photograph, so the imagery is generated or treated, not sourced.

Outline (this is a recurring FORMAT, not a single scroll; the "sequence" is the posting logic):
1. A sighting post is the ranch quietly showing you something it saw on its own land. It communicates that the place has real wildness in it, without narrating that as a selling point. Visual proof: the image itself carries everything; the words carry almost nothing.
2. The posts arrive irregularly and mixed into the normal feed, never grouped or captioned as a campaign. This communicates that the animal is not a mascot and the brand will not explain it. There is no visual chrome that says "brand post."
3. Across the series the same subject appears in different forms (a trail-camera frame, a formal portrait, an illustration), so a follower starts to recognize it without being told to. This communicates continuity and rewards attention.

Single point: the ranch has something wild living on it, and it treats that as an ordinary fact rather than a story to sell.

Feeling: quiet recognition that a real place has real wildness, plus a small conspiratorial pleasure at a brand that refuses to explain itself.

Tensions: the brand voice is warm and expansive, but the concept is deadpan and mostly silent. Resolution: where copy exists it is warm and plainly spoken like a neighbor's field note, and most of the time there is very little of it. The formality of the portrait form must never tip into labeling the animal a mascot.

## L3 — visual direction

Through-line: the sighting-as-record. Every post reads like the ranch logging what it saw, not advertising. The image is the whole design. The only "designed" chrome allowed is a small timestamp on the trail-camera form.

Devices: the burned-in timestamp (trail-cam form only); a single plain field-note line in the brand serif; the near-absence of brand chrome (a small wordmark, nothing more). No colored ground behind the animal (killed in round 2). No green anywhere (killed). No anchor rules or ticks (killed).

Units — the variation set is the three forms of the character:
- Form A, trail-camera capture: a night flash frame, black and white, one field note, a timestamp.
- Form B, studio anti-portrait: the wild animal given the formal, dignified portrait treatment on a plain warm ground, no caption, the deadpan doing the work.
- Form C, illustration: a contemporary painterly coyote, one field note about how often it turns up.

## L4 — copy (written to the voice, checked against L8)

- Form A field note: "The camera down by the low water crossing picked this up a little after three in the morning."
- Form B: no caption. A small wordmark only. The formal treatment carries it; explaining it would kill it.
- Form C field note: "We see one most weeks, usually right at the edge of the light."

All full sentences, warm and plainly spoken, no em dashes, numbers under eleven spelled out, no hype, no cleverness, never names the animal a mascot.
