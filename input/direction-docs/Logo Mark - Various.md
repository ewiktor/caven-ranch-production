Logo is being explored but hasn't landed. It tends to come last in this process because it needs to work alongside whatever the rest of the brand becomes. Until photography, color, and layout directions get further along, the logo can't fully resolve because you don't yet know what it has to sit next to.

### The Core Tension

There are two general territories in the references. One is organic and imperfect: wavy letterforms, irregular stroke weights, uneven baselines, hand-drawn character. The other is refined serif with subtle custom details: letterforms that are structurally clean but have some kind of architectural modification (concave terminals, softened edges, inward-curving stems) that makes them feel not-default.

The problem is that if the rest of the brand ends up sharp, rectilinear, and modern, a messy hand-drawn logo won't sit right. But if the brand has enough warmth and texture elsewhere, something more organic could work. That decision can't be made in isolation from everything else.

One exploration was a very messy scrawl. It was noted that in a context where everything else is sharp and composed, it felt wrong. Early AI-generated attempts produced basic serif forms with ink blotch details that weren't distinct enough. Nothing from the explorations so far has stuck.

### What the References Show

**Organic/imperfect territory:**

- A word mark with irregular fluid letterforms, sweeping terminals, varying stroke weights, and an uneven baseline. The waviness and un-uniformity of the letters is specifically what's liked about it.
- A monolinear script monogram (CR) where the letters integrate through a continuous gestural stroke. Consistent weight, rounded terminals. The interest here is in the script approach as a possible alternate mark, with a desire to try different takes on the exact script style.

**Refined serif territory:**

- A custom serif where the terminals have a hand-tooled quality, with subtle inward curvature on vertical stems and flared rounded ends. The commentary calls out the shape of the ends and caps specifically.
- A thin serif with concave notched indentations at the tops and bottoms of letterforms. Geometric and architectural, a modern modification on a classic serif structure.
- A soft serif with rounded terminals and softened edges in a warm desaturated cream color, shown over a full-bleed landscape. The softness of the edges, the color, and the lockup placement are all noted.

**Lockup and composition:**

- A stacked wordmark in wide-tracked all-caps with a secondary context line below at roughly 40% of the primary scale. The relative sizing and the lockup are the relevant parts, not the specific typeface.
- An asymmetric layout with type jammed into a corner, integrating handwriting into a normal paragraph. This relates more to how the logo lives in web compositions than to the mark itself.
