## What This Is

A spatial reference for anyone generating, reviewing, or prompting visual representations of this property. Grounded in confirmed satellite imagery, contour data, site plans with compass markers, architectural renders, and on-site photography. Ref images are stored in the reference bank under PROPERTY - MAP & LOCATION REFERENCES.

## The Shape of the Property

North up. Think of it as a wide body with a long narrow handle.

The main body expands to the west and northwest. This is where the land is: the dense woodland, the ridge, the river frontage, the big views. Most of the acreage is here.

The panhandle is a narrow strip reaching south from the main body down to the public road. The entry drive runs up this panhandle. You turn off the road, head north up the panhandle, pass through the gate at the southeast corner of the main body, and you're in.

The Pedernales River enters from the southwest, curves northward, and runs roughly west to east across the top of the property. It wraps around the west and north sides. Exposed white limestone bluffs and shelves. Teal-green water where it pools in the bends. The river creates a large area of land on the western and northern portions of the property, dense canopy and open fields bounded by the river's curve. This is where the river frontage is, where guests go to swim, where the flat limestone rocks and deep pools are.

Miller Creek is a smaller limestone drainage. It runs along the northeast edge of the ridge area and feeds into the Pedernales to the north. It creates a terrain break, a ravine condition, that defines the eastern edge of the buildable ridge.

## The Cabin Site

The development site sits on a ridge in the eastern portion of the main body. Not the center, not the far west. The eastern side, near where the panhandle meets the main body.

With north up in the site plan:

- **HOUSE** sits at the top (north) of the site on its own cleared pad, up on the higher ground. There's an octagonal or geometric footprint visible in aerials. That patch of driveway and clearing is the most readable man-made feature from above. It's the landmark for finding the site from any aerial angle.
- **GUEST** structure is just south of the house.
- **Unit A** (the first cabin) is on the west side of the site, past the gate. VIEW arrow points west.
- **Units B and C** step south from A. Each faces generally west to southwest, each with its own view corridor through existing clearings in the canopy.
- The **GATE** is roughly center-east of the site, where the road enters the cabin area.
- **Dense/tall tree coverage** fills the center and west of the immediate site area.
- **Private Plateau** is an open area to the east of the dense trees, within the site.

The house and its cleared pad sit a bit higher on the ridge than the cabins. You drive the road past the house area, loop around through the dense trees, and drop down slightly to reach the cabin positions on the western edge of the ridge.

## Topography of the Ridge

Contour data covers the ridge area specifically. The yellow contour rectangle in the reference images highlights this zone, the ridge itself, not the full property.

- **High point:** approximately 314 feet, toward the northwest of the contour area. This is on the upper bench where the house sits.
- **Low point:** approximately 282 feet, toward the southeast, down at the Miller Creek drainage.
- **Total change:** roughly 30 feet.
- The eastern slope toward Miller Creek is steep. Contour lines are tight and close together.
- The western slope toward the river valley is more gradual.

The cabin site sits toward the southeast corner of the contour rectangle, partway down from the high point, on the western edge of the ridge. The cabins face west, cantilevered out over the more gradual western slope. Behind them (uphill, east) is where the road, parking, and entry ramp are.

## What the Cabins See

The cabins face west. In front of them the terrain drops away gently. Dense continuous canopy, juniper and live oak, rolls out below and away from the ridge, stretching across the big western and northwestern portion of the property. Beyond that canopy, eventually, is the Pedernales valley and a distant flat horizon. This is the sunset view. The sky gets big here. The golden hour cabin photos show exactly this: the camera looking west over treetops to a long, flat, hazy horizon.

Between the cabin site and the river, there is a huge amount of land. That's the area the river curves around, dense woodland and fields. It's not a simple "the cabins overlook the river." There's a lot of landscape between the two.

## What the Land Looks Like

**Dense woodland** covers the majority of the property, especially the western and northern portions. Continuous canopy of Ashe juniper (dark, dense, rounded crowns) and live oak (lighter green, wider spread). From above it reads as a thick dark carpet. At ground level it's enclosed, the "tunnel of foliage" you drive through to reach the cabins. The canopy is dense enough that you can't see ground between the trees in most areas.

**More open land** exists in the eastern portion and along parts of the road approach. Scattered individual oaks with wide spreading canopies, golden-brown grass, visible ground, and more pastoral character. The transition between dense and open isn't a hard line.

**The river corridor** has its own character: exposed limestone, wide flat rock shelves, teal-green water pools, and bluffs. This is a different visual zone from both the dense woodland and the open meadow.

**White caliche roads** thread through the property and are visible in aerials as pale lines against the dark canopy. These are the primary way to find your way around from above.

## How to Orient from Any Angle

Reference images of this property come from different camera positions. The five things that stay constant:

1. **The Pedernales River** wraps the west and north. Always the big outer edge on those sides.
2. **Miller Creek** cuts through the northeast. Pale limestone drainage visible in aerials.
3. **The cleared house pad** is the geometric man-made clearing and patch driveway on the upper ridge. Most readable structure from above. Find this and you've found the site.
4. **The access road** is pale caliche lines threading through the canopy. Enters from the south (panhandle), reaches the site from the southeast (gate).
5. **The ridge drop** is steep to the east and northeast toward Miller Creek, gradual to the west. If you can tell which way the terrain falls, you know your orientation.

In the oblique views from the southeast (images 4 and 5 in the ref set): you're looking northwest. The river wraps around the far side, behind and beyond the ridge. Miller Creek is off to the right. The cabin site is on the ridge, partway into the frame. The cabins face away from you, looking west.

## The Cabin Structure

This matters for rendering because the form is specific and unusual.

- Pitched corrugated metal roof with exposed rafter tails
- Vertical cedar board-and-batten siding
- Dark red-brown steel framing, railings, and staircase
- Cantilevered off the west-facing slope on steel posts on concrete piers
- Limestone base where the cabin connects to the hillside on the uphill (east) side
- Glass walls on the west face, the view side
- Entry via a long metal railing ramp from the parking area on the uphill side
- Fire pit in the outdoor space

The cantilever pushes the front living space out west over the slope. The bedroom nestles back into the trees on the east/uphill side. The view opens up as you walk the ramp. You see it in front of you before you turn and enter.

From above at property scale: tiny. A small geometric footprint with a corrugated metal roof. It does not dominate anything.

## Scale

The property is enormous relative to the structures. The buildings are small. The land is vast. The dense canopy covers so much area that from most aerial views you wouldn't immediately spot the buildings. You'd find the road, follow it to the clearing, and find the structures there.

Any rendering where the structures feel like the main subject and the land feels like a backdrop has the relationship inverted.
