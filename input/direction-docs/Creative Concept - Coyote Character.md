An unexplained coyote that shows up across the brand in different forms. A studio photograph on one page, an illustration on another, an animation in the site preloader. It is never labeled, never announced as a mascot or brand animal. It just appears.

The idea came from trail cams. Hunters put cameras out on properties and they capture wildlife at night with high flash. The client had mentioned coyotes are on the property, and the thought was: what if one of them just keeps showing up across the brand in ways that don't quite make sense? The appeal is the tension between a brand that is otherwise grounded in 150 years of family history and this recurring wild animal that appears in controlled, composed, or absurd settings with no explanation.

The audience for this brand is Austin's creative and entrepreneurial scene. These are people in their 30s to 50s with money and taste who are drawn to brands that do something unexpected. The coyote is one way to give the brand an edge without making the whole identity edgy. If the rest of the brand is refined, serif type, considered photography, the coyote adds something that cuts against that in a way that's bold but not forced. 

### How It Shows Up

It is like an Easter egg that you start to notice as you move through the brand. The preloader animation could involve the coyote walking into frame. Another page might have an illustration of one.  A 404 page could feature it. The idea is that people who pay attention start to pick up on it, and that silent recognition becomes a connection point with the audience.

### Visual Forms Being Explored

There are several directions in the reference bank for how the coyote could be rendered, and the right answer is probably a mix depending on context.

**Studio photography:** The largest group of references. The coyote placed in a controlled studio setting, treated like a formal portrait subject. Several sub-directions exist within this:

- Clean, modern studio with soft directional lighting, monochromatic warm backgrounds. High-fashion animal portraiture with a deadpan quality.

**Hard rule: The coyote is never shown wearing clothing or human accessories.** The references that include a turtleneck or collar exist as photographic style and tone references only — they inform the lighting, framing, and deadpan quality of the shot, not the literal depiction. The coyote always appears as an actual coyote. No wardrobe, no anthropomorphization. The absurdity comes from placing a wild animal in unexpected contexts and treating it with the seriousness of a fashion portrait — not from dressing it up.

- Vintage or old-timey studio style with heavy film grain, sepia or black-and-white treatment, mottled or textured backdrops. The coyote treated like a dignified subject from an early 20th century encyclopedia or portrait sitting.
- Absurd framing within the studio context, where the crop is intentionally tight or off-center. An extreme close-up of just the snout against a saturated red background, or the top of the head and one ear filling the frame. These break the portrait convention and push the character into something more graphic.
- The coyote interacting with human-scale furniture or objects in the studio, like sitting behind a chair, which adds another layer of absurdity by placing a wild animal in a domestic context.

**High-flash and trail cam style:** Harsh direct flash, hard shadows, high contrast. The coyote caught mid-movement with motion blur, or staring into the camera from a high angle. This is the closest to the original trail cam idea that started the concept. It feels raw and candid, like the animal was genuinely caught on camera. The backgrounds in these references range from solid saturated colors to dark voids.

**Illustration:** Painterly, abstract, visible brushstrokes. The coyote rendered as an artistic interpretation rather than a realistic depiction. Some references use thick impasto texture with the coyote as a near-silhouette against a black void. 

The illustration approach avoids the concern about AI-generated photography feeling fake, because it is openly artistic rather than trying to pass as real. One reference uses an elongated, exaggerated silhouette with sharp vertical ears that gives the character a distinct graphic identity.

The old Western large-format camera style was explored but felt too old. That direction has been set aside.

### Concern About AI Detection

A real concern with the photographic versions is whether they look AI-generated. The studio portraits and trail cam shots need to not feel obviously synthetic. The illustration direction sidesteps this entirely because it's not trying to be photographic. For the photographic directions, the thinking is that putting the coyote in more real, grounded settings rather than overly polished or surreal ones helps. Texture and setting are the two things that tend to give away AI imagery, so getting those right matters.
