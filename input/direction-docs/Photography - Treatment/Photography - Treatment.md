## Photography: Treatment and Lighting

This is only about treatment: how images look and feel. Not what to shoot (composition and concept doc) or how to frame it. The purpose of this doc is to hold the different lighting, color grading, post-production texture, and image treatment directions being explored so that AI chats helping with image generation can read it and produce prompts in the right territory.

These directions are not decided. They are separate territories on the table, surfaced through reference exploration. Each one is its own distinct direction with its own character. They should be treated independently when generating imagery, not blended or merged together. The point of keeping them separate is to be able to test each one cleanly and see what it looks like applied to the brand's subject matter without contamination from the others.

The client leans toward warmer treatments. The creative direction leans toward something cleaner and more technical, partly because pushing warmth too far risks the brand feeling like a vibey hippie place rather than a refined ranch property. That tension hasn't been resolved yet. They may coexist for different contexts, or one may win out.

Whatever direction gets chosen, consistency matters. The mechanism for keeping treatment consistent across AI-generated imagery is keywords. If the right terms are identified and used reliably in prompts, the output stays in line. That's why each territory below includes promptable language: the specific words and phrases that, when included in a generation prompt, pull the output toward that territory.

---

### Territory 1: Warm Golden Haze

Amber warmth, atmospheric bloom, nostalgic. Soft highlight roll-off, lifted shadows, low contrast.

**Promptable language:**

- warm golden hour light with orange-gold highlights on tree canopies
- strong atmospheric haze with golden hour light bloom and soft highlight roll-off
- warm amber color grade with low contrast and lifted shadows for a filmic look
- fine film grain texture with organic post-production editing treatment
- dreamy nostalgic vibe with diffused sunlight and soft focus depth
- hazy atmospheric perspective with a warm amber tint in the shadows
- soft sun-drenched image treatment with low contrast and warm midtones
- heavy atmospheric haze with soft highlight bloom and lifted blacks
- warm golden hour lighting with amber and honey color grading
- muted earth tones with a warm color temperature overlay

One reference of this direction has "more orange warmth" compared to a cooler treatment of the same scene. There's uncertainty about which way to go between this and the alternatives.

---

### Territory 2: Muted Earthy Film

Desaturated palette, overcast quality, serene. Fine grain, matte finish, low contrast. Quieter than the warm directions.

**Promptable language:**

- muted earthy color palette with desaturated greens and warm ochre tones
- fine film grain texture with a tactile, analog photography aesthetic
- soft overcast lighting with flat contrast and no harsh shadows
- editorial post-production treatment with a nostalgic, cinematic film look
- serene and quiet atmosphere with a naturalist, organic mood
- heavy film grain post-production with a matte, low-contrast finish
- muted golden hour lighting with soft diffused highlights and desaturated sky
- nostalgic, melancholic atmosphere with a vintage film aesthetic

---

### Territory 3: High-Clarity Golden Hour

Clean, rich, sharp. No haze, no atmospheric softness. Full digital fidelity with deep saturation and crisp shadow definition.

**Promptable language:**

- ultra-clear digital photography with high micro-contrast and zero atmospheric haze
- sharp golden hour sunburst with defined light rays and minimal lens flare
- rich textural detail in foreground meadow and architectural materials
- deep color saturation with high dynamic range and crisp shadow definition
- layered landscape composition with sharp focus from foreground to distant hills

This was described as "a little bit of depth, richer digital clarity" and positioned explicitly as an alternative to the hazier treatments. "I don't think we want to go too haze on this, but this is another direction."

---

### Territory 4: Cinematic Western Textured Film

Heavy character. Vintage 35mm feel, monochromatic leanings, painterly. Dense grain, low contrast, atmospheric depth.

**Promptable language:**

- heavy film grain texture, vintage 35mm aesthetic, tactile post-production finish
- warm golden hour monochromatic grade, amber and ochre color palette, soft diffused highlights
- soft hazy lighting, diffused sun through smoke or clouds, low contrast shadows
- muted desaturated color grading with a cinematic film grain texture and low contrast
- soft diffused dusk lighting with a cool blue hour color temperature and no harsh shadows

This overlaps with Territory 1 in some ways but goes heavier on grain and further toward monochrome. There's an awareness that this Western cinematic look is heavily used in the boutique hotel space. Earlier explorations in this direction felt like every other Western hotel brand.

---

### Territory 5: Dappled Natural Light

Soft, filtered through canopy or architectural elements. Two variants emerged: one gentler and one more dramatic.

**Promptable language (gentle variant):**

- soft dappled sunlight filtering through dense tree canopy
- muted naturalistic color palette with earthy wood tones and sage greens
- low-contrast filmic aesthetic with fine grain and soft highlights
- architectural photography style emphasizing organic integration with environment
- backlit foliage creating a soft ethereal glow around the subject

**Promptable language (dramatic variant):**

- dramatic volumetric god rays piercing through dense foliage at golden hour
- heavy film grain texture with vintage 35mm aesthetic
- backlit tall meadow grass with high-contrast golden highlights
- hazy atmospheric perspective with low-lying morning mist
- desaturated blue sky contrasting with warm amber light leaks

The gentle variant was called out as "one of my favorites" as a distinct direction. Both are about the quality of light being shaped by the environment before it reaches the subject.

---

### Territory 6: Interior Light Play

The treatment specific to interiors. Defined by how exterior light enters and interacts with interior surfaces. Angled shadows, subtle warmth on materials, the exterior going hazy or overexposed through glass.

**Promptable language:**

- sharp angled shadows cast across the floor from large floor-to-ceiling windows
- hazy overexposed white exterior landscape visible through glass
- subtle warm interior glow with soft matte post-production texture
- desaturated earthy tones with a filmic, hazy atmosphere
- tactile lime-wash wall texture with soft diffused highlights

The subtlety of the interior warmth was specifically emphasized: "angled shadows pouring in and a bit of soft warmth inside VERY SUBTLE, but a bit white and hazy outside."

---

### Territory 7: Nighttime Warm Tungsten

High contrast between warm light sources (fire, interior lamps) and deep twilight or darkness. The warm-cool contrast is the defining feature of this territory.

**Promptable language:**

- warm firelight glow against deep twilight blue sky, high contrast warm and cool tones
- cinematic low-light photography, natural light sources, flickering orange highlights on faces and wooden deck
- warm amber tungsten light spill, high contrast night photography, deep shadows with rich blacks
- cinematic film grain, 35mm aesthetic
- low-light atmosphere, golden hour afterglow in the background, moody and intimate evening tone
- subjects silhouetted against warm interior light, backlighting emphasizing form and texture

This territory was noted twice with similar language in the references. It's specifically about how to capture night images and the depth of lighting, the scale of lighting, and mood tone.

---

### How These Relate to Each Other

Each territory above is a separate direction. When generating imagery to test a territory, use only the promptable language from that territory. The point is to see each one in isolation before making decisions about which to carry forward.

The warmer, hazier directions (Territory 1, Territory 4) and the cleaner direction (Territory 3) are the main fork. Both are on the table.

Territory 6 (interiors) and Territory 7 (nighttime) have their own light character by nature of their context. They will apply regardless of which daytime exterior territory wins. How rigid the boundaries are between the daytime exterior territories is open.

The amount of film grain varies significantly across territories, from fine and barely there to heavy vintage 35mm. Whether there's a baseline grain level or each territory sets its own hasn't been pinned down.

## Tonal Range — Global Rule

Across all territories, the tonal range needs to stay contained. Shadows should not crush to black — keep detail and openness in the darks. Highlights should not blow out to pure white — pull them back so there's still information in the brightest areas. The overall image should feel like it lives in a comfortable middle dynamic range: lifted shadows, restrained highlights, nothing extreme on either end.

This came out of testing Territory 5/6 interior work. The direction was right but the shadows went too dark and the highlights washed out too far. The fix is subtle — just keep the tonal endpoints from going to their extremes.

When prompting, add language like:

- lifted shadows with visible detail in the darkest areas
- restrained highlights, no pure white blowout
- contained dynamic range, midtone-focused exposure

This applies to every territory regardless of its other characteristics.

## What's Unresolved

- There's a tension between the warmer, hazier directions (Territories 1, 4) and the cleaner, sharper direction (Territory 3). Both are on the table. They may coexist for different contexts or one may win out.
- The amount of film grain varies significantly across territories — from fine and barely there to heavy vintage 35mm. Whether there's a baseline grain level or each territory sets its own hasn't been addressed.
- Some territories naturally overlap or blend. Interior shots (Territory 6) will have their own light character regardless of whether the overall brand leans warm or clean. Nighttime (Territory 7) is its own thing by necessity. How rigid the boundaries are between the daytime exterior territories is open.
